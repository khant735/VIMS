Vulkan Image Mask Studio v0.4.12.12

The supplied v0.4.11.0 run showed a neck-only main face model, five detailed-model masks, including a crossed-side eyebrow and a 40-pixel teeth mask with 0% mouth containment. This build keeps the v0.4.11.1 face crop and validates specialist eye, brow, and mouth masks against the detected face and visible contrast support. It rejects undersized or misplaced specialist outputs and rejects teeth outside the mouth. The lower-confidence fallback fills missing parts. It is still a general method, with no image-specific data or imports.

Use the full ZIP for a new install, or replace the executable in an existing install with matching runtime DLLs and installed ONNX models. Inspect the on-screen face-model counts, Logs/image_properties.log, and exported masks. This build was cross-compiled and ZIP-checked but not run under Windows here.

Mask area guidance: Select a detected mask, tick Edit mask area, drag inside the cyan box to move it, drag the edges or corners to resize, or drag outside to draw a new box. Apply area clips that mask outside the chosen rectangle and saves the original image, original predicted mask, and rough rectangle metadata under TrainingGuides. This is weak supervision only; it does not verify pixels inside, create new detectors, or retrain ONNX models. Raw view remains the unmodified model prediction. Analyse again to reset edits.

Rotation: Tick Edit mask area. Drag the cyan circle connected above the rectangle to rotate it, or hold Alt while dragging inside it. Edges and corners resize in the rectangle's rotated axes. Apply area clips the mask using the visible rotated rectangle and records rotation_degrees in the rough guide metadata. Reset area clears the angle. When the rotation handle is outside the image viewport, use Alt + drag.

Nostrils: When the nose detector yields two separate credible dark details, the tree exposes Nose/Nostrils/Left nostril and Right nostril, alongside the combined Nostrils parent mask. The sides use detected eye positions when available; otherwise a frontal-facing orientation assumption is marked at lower confidence. One dark shadow or an ambiguous nose produces no left/right nostril prediction. The face ONNX model does not have nostril classes; these are approximate derived regions.

Face regions: Cheeks now exposes Left cheek and Right cheek beneath its existing combined mask. Cheekbones and Eyelids each expose a combined mask with distinct left and right children. Side assignment uses both detected eye masks. Cheeks are split from visible face pixels; cheekbones are approximate upper-cheek regions; eyelids are approximate visible face pixels adjacent to the corresponding eye. When both eyes or sufficient pixels are missing, the relevant pair remains unavailable. No dedicated cheekbone or eyelid ONNX detector is installed.

Eyelashes: When both corresponding eye and eyelid masks exist and thin dark upper-edge pixel candidates are present on both sides, Eyelashes exposes Left eyelash and Right eyelash masks beneath a combined parent. Low-confidence derived candidates only; there is no trained eyelash ONNX detector. If either candidate is absent or ambiguous, both remain unavailable.

Saved TrainingGuides: The analyser automatically loads rough area guides from TrainingGuides when analysing the exact same decoded image again. It selects the newest guide per mask name, clips the refined mask (and its descendants) outside the saved rotated area, updates measurements, and reports the applied guide count. The Raw view remains the unmodified model output. Earlier schema-1 guides are compared against their stored PNG; new schema-2 guides include an image SHA-256 fingerprint. Guides do not automatically train ONNX models or generalize to different images. Keep the TrainingGuides folder alongside the executable when updating the application.

Mask tree: the Detected subjects and parts list contains only detected masks and unavailable mask parts. Per-mask bounding boxes, areas, containment and side-overlap statistics continue to be computed internally and written to Logs/mask_measurements.csv; they no longer appear as rows in the mask tree.

APPROVED LOCAL LEARNING (experimental)
Select a detected mask and tick Edit mask area. Shift+drag over the image adds mask pixels; Ctrl+drag removes them. Hold Shift and roll the mouse wheel over the image to set brush radius (1-64 image pixels). Edit and apply the movable, resizable, rotatable area as before. For a missing part in the tree, select its (no detector) row and press Create missing mask, then paint its pixels. Review the whole mask and press Approve mask for learning. This explicitly saves the full image, approved pixel mask, original prediction and parent region in TrainingSamples beside the executable; it also replays the approved mask on the same image. A saved rough TrainingGuide alone is NEVER treated as an approved pixel mask.
After three distinct approved images of the same part and parent, a small CPU pixel neural head trains on all but the newest image and must improve intersection-over-union by at least 0.03, reach IoU >=0.60, and precision >=0.75 on that held-out approved image before its model is activated. It is a local companion model, not a modification of the .onnx weights. Accepted models in LearnedModels supplement missing parts and refine predictions within a detected parent on different images. If an existing learned model regresses on a new approved image, it is paused; its file is retained with a .paused suffix. The raw view still shows the original model prediction. Results on real images are unverified until you test them. Keep TrainingGuides, TrainingSamples and LearnedModels next to the updated application when moving folders. Training occurs only from user-approved masks; analysing an uncorrected image cannot establish ground truth.

0.4.12.6: Approved-mask pixel learner now considers brightness independent observed colour, local shadow ratio and edge contrast. Existing V1 learned models load with their original six features preserved. A bounded skin refinement follows local colour across gentle shading inside detected anatomy, excluding clothing and opposing limbs. Pixel observations do not determine intrinsic pigmentation.

0.4.12.6: Colour, shading and shadow boundary refinement now applies to all detected masks with suitable seed pixels, including face details, clothing, body parts, animals and scene regions. Uses nearby colour continuity, an existing parent mask and competing sibling masks. No new semantic class is inferred from shading alone. Raw ONNX masks remain available.

0.4.12.6: Face parsing landmarks estimate in-image roll and possible profile views. Rotate fallback facial regions, align weak fallback eye/nose/mouth regions to visible landmarks, and avoid inventing an unseen opposite eye in one-eye profile candidates. Head and body groups also remain available when hair is detected from behind without a face. Pose is an image-space hint rather than a true 3D head rotation estimate.

0.4.12.6: Rotate approximate parent-relative part regions with detected mask geometry; split broad body regions along the person silhouette rather than screen rows. The approved-mask learner gains orientation-normalized parent coordinates for all labels; V1 and V2 learned models load with original weights unchanged and new inputs zero-weighted until validated retraining. Existing ONNX output remains the baseline. Highly symmetric, hidden or ambiguous silhouettes do not yield a reliable rotation estimate.

0.4.12.6: Added low-confidence, silhouette-anchored 2D projections for hidden front/rear/opposite/side surfaces of detected people, animals and vehicles. Tree and preview clearly distinguish projected masks from visible pixels; machine measurements mark projected_hidden. Projected entries cannot be approved as training labels and never export source-image cutouts. Image-left/right side labels are screen-relative, not anatomical identification. No true unseen texture, shape or 3D geometry can be reconstructed from the single image.

0.4.12.6: Export 30-second pose GIFs from the analysed image and its visible masks, one transparent 320x320 GIF per detected human or animal in Exports/PoseGIFs. 240 frames at 8 fps (alternating 120/130ms) with a repeating 30-second cycle. Human arms, head and legs use available masks to rotate around approximate joints; images with missing limbs and whole-animal-only masks use visibly approximate motion. Pose GIFs are simple 2D cutout puppets, not 3D reconstructions, true inverse kinematics, or additional detectors. Projected hidden surfaces are excluded.

0.4.12.7: The right controls scroll vertically (mouse wheel over empty panel or its scrollbar), making the GIF exporter reachable on short windows. Opened images start fitted to the preview; the original image stays at full resolution for analysis and export. Use the + and - magnifier buttons, Fit image, or the mouse wheel over the preview to zoom about the cursor; hold Shift and use the wheel in Edit mask area to adjust paint radius. The preview is resampled bicubically when resized or zoomed. Dragging a mask area and painting use matching coordinates at every zoom.

GIF: Press Export 30-second pose GIFs after analysis. For one subject, choose its .gif save path in the Save As dialog; when multiple subjects exist, exports go to Exports/PoseGIFs. GIFs are 1280x720, 240 frames at 8 fps and retain the original image aspect ratio in a transparent 16:9 canvas. Enlarged masks and their source pixels use bicubic sampling. Export runs on the UI thread and may temporarily make the window unresponsive on large or numerous subjects.

Learning approval: New approved samples use short label-derived directory names; earlier TrainingSamples folders are also consulted. PNG saving uses an extended absolute Windows path and now reports the HRESULT and path length on failure. The exact cause of the reported failure cannot be verified without reproducing it on the affected Windows install.

0.4.12.8: Fixed the scrollable right panel drawing stale copies of controls during scrollbar movement. Scrolling now places all controls from their original coordinates in a single layout update and redraws them together. Increased the readable width and spacing of crowded buttons and checkboxes. The main window opens maximized, with a fixed size and only minimize and close controls on its title bar. System task switching is managed by Windows.

0.4.12.9: Preview zoom keeps the image within the viewport and restores exact centering at Fit or the minimum wheel zoom. Wheel zoom follows the pointer while keeping the image inside the visible bounds. The Fit and magnifier buttons live in a dedicated strip above the Vulkan image surface, so image rendering cannot paint over them.

0.4.12.10: Drag the image with the left mouse button while mask editing is off; use the middle mouse button to pan at any time, including during mask editing. The image stays within the viewport, so panning has no effect while the full image already fits. The right controls now live on one clipped content window moved as a whole while scrolling; a separate 32-DPI-pixel vertical scrollbar provides a thicker target.

0.4.12.11: Restores the standard Windows top-level window style used before capture problems were reported. The window still starts maximized; resizing, moving and restoring it into a smaller window remain disabled. The maximize/restore title control can appear disabled to maintain a conventional capturable frame; minimize and close remain available. This is a targeted compatibility change for Windows Snipping Tool, requiring verification on the affected Windows installation.

0.4.12.12: Replaced the large partially painted refinement group box with a small header. The group's interior previously retained images of controls that passed beneath it while scrolling. The right panel now composes its child controls into a buffer and explicitly redraws the content surface after movement. No compositing is applied to the Vulkan preview.
